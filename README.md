# Manosmita Giri

**BS–MS Physics Student | Solar Physics | Computational Astrophysics | Radiative MHD**

I am a fifth-year BS–MS Physics student at the **Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Berhampur, India**, currently undertaking my master's thesis at the **Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA)**.

My research interests lie at the intersection of solar physics, magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), radiative transfer, and computational astrophysics. I am particularly interested in understanding the dynamics of the solar atmosphere through numerical simulations, forward modelling, and the synthesis and interpretation of observable diagnostics.

My current thesis work focuses on **forward modelling Hα line-wing diagnostics from radiative MHD simulation data**, with an emphasis on optical-depth diagnostics and the interpretation of atmospheric structures. Through this work, I am developing experience in Python-based scientific analysis, numerical data processing, and the visualization of simulation-derived physical quantities.

I am interested in further developing these skills through doctoral research in solar physics, computational plasma astrophysics, and related areas.

## Current Research

### Forward Modelling of Hα Line-Wing Diagnostics from Radiative MHD Simulation Data

*Master's thesis project — Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA)*

This project explores the use of radiative MHD simulation data to investigate Hα line-wing diagnostics of structures in the solar atmosphere.

The analysis focuses on constructing simplified line-wing diagnostics and examining how opacity, optical depth, atmospheric velocity, density, and temperature contribute to the formation and interpretation of synthetic diagnostic signals.

**Research themes**
- Analysis of radiative MHD simulation variables.
- Construction and interpretation of optical-depth diagnostics.
- Investigation of the height dependence of line-wing formation.
- Python-based analysis and visualization of simulation data.
- Forward modelling as a connection between simulations and observations.
